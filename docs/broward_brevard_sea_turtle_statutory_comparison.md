# Broward vs. Brevard County: Sea Turtle Nesting Conservation Laws

> **Note:** This overview was written from general knowledge of Florida's legal framework, not from statute text in this folder. Check the specific local code sections and dates on Municode before relying on them.

## The same rules apply in both counties

Federal and state law are the same in Broward and Brevard. The legal differences between the two counties come from local ordinances and how each county runs its program.

| Level | Authority | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Federal | Endangered Species Act (16 U.S.C. §1531 et seq.) | Lists loggerheads and greens as threatened, and leatherbacks, hawksbills and Kemp's ridleys as endangered. Harming ("take") any of them is prohibited. |
| State | Marine Turtle Protection Act, s. 379.2431 F.S. | Makes it illegal to disturb turtles, nests or eggs. Nest work requires an FWC permit. |
| State | Rule 68E-1 F.A.C. (FWC) | Sets the rules for marine turtle permits, such as nest marking, relocation and hatchling releases. |
| State | s. 161.163 F.S. and Ch. 62B-55 F.A.C. | Has the state identify nesting beaches and gives local governments a model lighting ordinance to adopt. |
| State | Coastal Construction Control Line program (s. 161.053 F.S., Ch. 62B-33 F.A.C.) | Sets lighting and timing conditions for permits to build near the beach. |
| Federal/State | USFWS consultations and state beach permits for sand placement | Require turtle monitoring and limit when beach renourishment can happen. |

## Where the two counties differ

| Topic | Brevard | Broward |
|---|---|---|
| **Who regulates lighting** | Two layers: a county ordinance covers unincorporated beachfront, and each beach town (Melbourne Beach, Indialantic, Satellite Beach, Cocoa Beach, Cape Canaveral and others) has its own. | Almost all beachfront is inside cities, so lighting rules are mostly city ordinances (Fort Lauderdale, Hollywood, Pompano Beach, Deerfield Beach, Hillsboro Beach and others). |
| **Ordinance season** | The county ordinance has generally covered **May 1 – Oct 31**. | City ordinances generally cover **March 1 – Oct 31**. The earlier start fits leatherbacks, which begin nesting earlier in southeast Florida. |
| **Federal land** | Large federal holdings change the rules on much of the coast. Archie Carr National Wildlife Refuge (south Brevard into Indian River County) is one of the most important loggerhead nesting areas in the Western Hemisphere. Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Kennedy Space Center and Canaveral National Seashore follow federal lighting plans and ESA consultations. | Very little federal beachfront, so local ordinances and state permits do most of the work. |
| **Monitoring and permits** | Many FWC permit holders (universities, nonprofits, federal agencies, towns) each cover different stretches of beach. | The county runs one coordinated program for nearly the whole coastline under FWC permits. |
| **Nest management** | Lighting is darker overall, so most nests can be left where they are. | Heavy urban lighting has historically meant more nest relocation and more disoriented hatchlings. FWC has tightened relocation rules over time. |
| **Beach cleaning and renourishment** | Renourishment projects are mostly managed for dune and shoreline protection. | Frequent renourishment and mechanical beach raking are common. Permits usually require morning nest surveys and staked nest buffers before any equipment goes on the sand. |
| **How strictly lighting rules work in practice** | Low-density towns make compliance easier. Rules for exterior fixtures (shielding, height, long-wavelength amber lighting) are straightforward to meet. | High-rise and commercial beachfronts make compliance harder. Cities rely more on shielding, tinted windows, and lighting conditions attached to building permits. |

## What the data in this folder shows

- **[municipality_turtle_comparison_dataset.csv](municipality_turtle_comparison_dataset.csv):** every city listed in both counties has a lighting ordinance. So the real differences are in what those ordinances say (season dates, enforcement, building permit conditions), not whether one exists.
- **[broward_brevard_beach_geography_turtle_comparison.csv](broward_brevard_beach_geography_turtle_comparison.csv):** Brevard had about 53,000 loggerhead and green nests in 2025 on 71.6 miles of beach, compared with about 3,300 in Broward on 24 miles.
- **[beach_access_hours_comparison (1).csv](beach_access_hours_comparison%20(1).csv):** Brevard parks listed show no night access, while Broward parks show limited night access. This affects how much people disturb the beach at night, even though it isn't a turtle-specific law.

## Where to verify

1. **Municode:** search "sea turtle" in the Brevard County Code and in each city's code.
2. **FWC:** the Marine Turtle Permit holder list and local ordinance list.
3. **FDEP:** the Ch. 62B-55 model ordinance, to compare each local ordinance against it.
